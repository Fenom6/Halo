Pachelbel's Canon

The opening of the Canon in D (around 1680), computed from the score on the spot — not a recording. Written more than 300 years ago, it is free for anyone to use.

The bass repeats eight notes (D · A · B · F♯ · G · D · G · A). Above it, three voices sing the same melody, each starting two bars (8 beats) after the last. On the halo, three lights follow the three voices and a gold ring follows the bass.

How Halo works

In Japanese temples, the ring of light behind a Buddha statue is called a kōhai — a halo. This app arranges pitch on a ring of light.

Reading it

Direction
The note name. Like a clock face: C is at 12 o'clock, then one semitone per hour clockwise (C♯ at 1, D at 2 … A at 9, B at 11). The twelve notes fit exactly on the twelve hours.
Distance from center
The octave. Low notes are inside, high notes outside. One turn of the spiral is one octave (innermost: C2, about 65 Hz; after 7 turns, outermost: C9, about 8,400 Hz).
Length and color of rays
How strong that pitch is. A thousand rays reach out from the spiral.
Vermilion light
The pitch right now. Two faint lights follow the same path, each 0.8 s later.
Center
The current note name (○ when there is no clear pitch). Touch the picture to see the pitch at that spot.

Physics: pitch and harmonics

Sound is air vibrating. How many times it vibrates per second is its frequency (in Hz, hertz); more vibrations sound higher. A vibrates 440 times a second. Twice that, 880, also sounds like A — one octave up. That is why the halo puts notes an octave apart in the same direction.

Voices and instruments also contain sounds at 2×, 3×, 4× … the frequency (harmonics). Play a single C with "Touch": 2× and 4× light up toward C, 3× toward G (7 o'clock) and 5× toward E (4 o'clock). C–E–G sounds good together because a single note already contains it.

Bells don't follow whole-number multiples, so their rays don't line up — that is their distinctive ring.

Math: the spiral

turn = log2(f ÷ 65.4)

f
frequency of the sound [Hz]
65.4
frequency of the innermost C (C2) [Hz]
log2
"how many times do you multiply 2 to get this number". When f doubles, the turn grows by exactly 1 (one turn = one octave)
whole part
which ring (0, 1, 2 … from the inside)
fractional part
the direction: 0.25 is a quarter turn = 3 o'clock

Example: A at 440 Hz gives log2(440 ÷ 65.4) ≈ 2.75 — two and three quarter turns, so 9 o'clock on ring 3. A semitone is the twelfth root of 2 (about 1.0595×). Multiply twelve times and you get exactly 2, so twelve semitones make one turn.

Music: canon (a round)

A canon is music where the same melody is sung again a little later by another voice, like "Row, Row, Row Your Boat". With the melody x(t) and the delay T:

y(t) = x(t) + x(t − T) + x(t − 2T)

In Pachelbel's Canon T is two bars (8 beats). At every moment the three voices are singing different parts of the melody, yet they fit perfectly over the eight chords of the bass. The three lights chasing along the same path are exactly that "delayed overlap".

Sing the bass (D · A · B · F♯ · G · D · G · A) or the start of the melody (F♯ · E · D · C♯ · B · A · B · C♯) into the microphone, or play it by touch, and… Any starting note works — it listens to how many semitones each step moves.

Music around the world

The button under the halo (the globe) lets you match four conventions.

Reference pitch
How many Hz A4 is. The international standard is 440 Hz, but many orchestras in Japan and Europe tune to 442 Hz, and Baroque music often uses 415 Hz. Heard against 442 Hz, a 440 Hz tone reads about 8 cents flat (1 cent = 1/100 of a semitone).
Note names
The same pitch has different names in different places: Do Re Mi in Japanese kana, C D E (English), German (B is H and B♭ is B), fixed Do Re Mi (France, Italy, Latin America…), movable do, Indian sargam (Sa Re Ga) and numbered notation (1 2 3) used in China and elsewhere.
Tonic at 12 o'clock
The spiral turns so the chosen note is at the top. Put the tonic of a song, or Sa in Indian music, at 12 o'clock and every key shows its scale as the same shape. With movable do, sargam and jianpu this note becomes Do, Sa or 1.
Quarter tones
Half a semitone (50 cents). Arabic, Turkish and Persian music use steps smaller than a semitone. Turn this on to name the note halfway between two semitones, like “E−¼” (a quarter tone below E), and draw thin half-hour lines on the halo.

Math: changing the reference from 440 to a multiplies every frequency by a ÷ 440. In cents that is 1200 × log2(a ÷ 440). For 442: 1200 × log2(442 ÷ 440) ≈ 7.9 cents higher.

Code: how it is drawn

  1. An AnalyserNode (Web Audio API) splits the incoming sound with an FFT (fast Fourier transform) into 8,192 frequency bins (each about 2.9 Hz wide). Low notes sit inside the halo, and they need fine bins or the rays fan out and blur the chord shapes (the price: the rays move a little slower).
  2. Each of the thousand rays knows in advance which bins it covers (armBinMap). Low rays interpolate between bins; high rays take the largest value in their range.
  3. About 60 times a second, the length and color of every ray is computed and drawn. Rays are grouped into ten brightness buckets and drawn per bucket, which keeps it fast (Path2D).
  4. About every 0.08 s the current pitch is measured (the YIN method, from how the wave repeats). The two faint lights read that history with a delay.

What it can and can't do

  • The center shows a note name only for sounds with a clear pitch (voice, instruments, whistling). For chords it shows the strongest component.
  • Notes below C2 (about 65 Hz) or above C9 (about 8,400 Hz) are off the spiral and not drawn (choosing another tonic shifts this range by up to half an octave).
  • Note names use the 12 semitones (24 with quarter tones on). Notes off that grid, such as just intonation, Indian shruti or gamelan scales, are shown as the nearest name plus how many cents away.
  • Sound is only processed on this device. Nothing is recorded or sent.
  • On iPhone, "Canon" and "Touch" may be silent when the ring/silent switch is set to silent.
